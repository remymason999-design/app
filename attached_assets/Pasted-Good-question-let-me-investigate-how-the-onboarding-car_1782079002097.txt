Here's the answer to your question:

Yes — onboarding cards are personalised, and the system learns from them correctly
1. The cards are personalised ✅
When I onboarded a test user picking Horror + Sci-Fi (Netflix + Prime), the 18 taste-check cards came back 78% matching their chosen genres, with ~22% deliberate variety from outside their picks (to avoid pigeonholing). The deck is built from the user's selected genres and their service/content-type filters — not a fixed generic list. Cards already seen/rated are excluded so they never repeat.

2. The recommendation system learns from each swipe ✅
After rating those cards, the user's learned profile updated immediately and sensibly:

Genres: Sci-Fi +6.3, Action +3.8, Adventure +1.3 (liked) vs Comedy −1.0 (disliked) — liked genres go strongly positive, disliked go negative.
Tone: dark +1.5, light −0.45 — it's learning how you want to feel, not just genre.
Type: movies vs TV both tracked.
Plus a gentle cold-start seed (+1.0) on any picked genre you didn't happen to swipe on, so the first feed is personalised even before swiping.
3. The first Discover feed reflects it ✅
The opening feed came back relevant — Sci-Fi well represented, with the variety band intact.

One thing worth flagging (not a bug — supply)
Horror is thin for the Netflix/Prime combo — only ~1 Horror title surfaced in the onboarding deck and ~4 in the first feed. That's a catalogue depth characteristic for that genre/provider mix, not a learning fault. If Horror is a genre you expect users to lean on heavily at launch, deepening Horror import coverage would help; otherwise the engine is doing the right thing with the titles available.

Net: personalisation and learning are both working correctly. No code changes were needed here — this was a verification pass.
